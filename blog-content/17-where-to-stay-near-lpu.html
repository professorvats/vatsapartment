New students ask this question every admission season, and the internet answers with ads. Your choice of zone decides your rent, your commute and a surprising amount of your daily mood.

The Best Place to Stay Near LPU, by Student Type

The first-year student

Choose: campus side (Apna Chai Wala) or Lawgate. You'll have daily classes, orientation events and new friends everywhere. A walkable room — like Vats Apartment's 10-minute-walk rooms from ₹9,000 — means you never miss anything and never stress about late-night transport.

The budget-focused senior

Choose: Lawgate shared rooms, or twin-share a furnished room. Sharing a ₹9,000 Vats room with a friend is ₹4,500 each — near-campus rent at Phagwara prices, with a private bathroom instead of a shared floor one. Our sharing guide covers the details.

The privacy-first student

Choose: a furnished private room on the campus side. No curfew, private bathroom and kitchen, your own Smart TV and desk — this is exactly the Vats setup, with rooftop deluxe rooms at ₹14,000 for the full upgrade.

The block-schedule student

Choose: Phagwara town or the Phagwara Road stretch. If you're only on campus 3–4 days a week, cheaper rent out east can make sense — just read the Phagwara trade-offs first.

The One Question That Decides Everything

Before comparing rents, answer this: how many days a week will you be on campus? 5+ days → stay walkable (zones 1–2). 3–4 days → the cheaper zones open up. Anything else is overthinking it. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Where do LPU students live mostly?

A: In the student zones along the campus — the Apna Chai Wala/main-gate pocket and the Lawgate belt — with a smaller share in Phagwara town and the Phagwara Road stretch.

Q: What's the best place to stay near LPU for first-year students?

A: Within walking distance of campus. First year is when campus life is densest — a 10-minute-walk room (Vats starts at ₹9,000, ₹4,500 shared) pays for itself in missed-nothing mornings.

Q: Is it worth paying more to live closer to LPU?

A: For most students, yes — transport savings plus 20+ hours a month of time returned. Only students with sparse class schedules genuinely benefit from cheaper, farther areas.
